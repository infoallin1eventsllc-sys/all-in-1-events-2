The real fault is underneath the typos. The mark is different in all four: different letterforms, different leaf placement, different colour logic. A generator draws a new logo every time you ask. A brand needs the same one every time, which is what a vector file is for.

Construction

Five rules that hold it together.

01
The leaf never touches a character

It crowns the numerals with clear space above them. That single decision is what makes "420" unbreakable — the failure in three of the four mockups was a leaf sitting on the 2.

02
FRIENDLY is a bar, not loose type

Reversed out of a solid block at 0.30 em tracking. The block gives the lockup a base, survives a bad embroidery digitisation, and reads at chest size across a room.

03
Two colours, maximum

Green and one neutral. Every extra thread colour is a real line item on an embroidery quote and a screen on a print quote.

04
It has to work in one colour

Single-colour versions below. If a mark only survives in full colour it fails on a woven label, an embossed box and a one-colour sleeve hit.

05
Below 20 mm the leaf comes off

Serrations fill in at small sizes and turn to mush. The small lockup drops the leaf and keeps the words, which is the half that carries the name.

On red, drop the green. Green on red is the lowest-contrast pair on the wheel and the two fight at any distance. The reversed one-colour version is the red garment's version — not a compromise, the correct choice.
