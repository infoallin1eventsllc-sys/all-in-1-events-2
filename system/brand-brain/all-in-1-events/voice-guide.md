# All in 1 Events — Voice

Warm, professional, upscale — but specific. The old one-line brief ("warm,
professional, upscale") produced drafts that could have come from any venue in
the country. This is the version with edges.

## How it sounds

Like a competent person who has run four hundred events and is not anxious
about running yours. Calm, concrete, unhurried. The reassurance comes from
detail, not from adjectives.

- Good: "We'll be there at 4 to set up, and the floor is clear by midnight."
- Bad: "We provide exceptional, seamless service for your special day!"

## Vocabulary

Say: the night, the floor, the room, load-in, run of show, your date.
Say what things are: lighting, photo booth, DJ, uplighting, dance floor.

Avoid: "unforgettable", "magical", "dream", "bespoke", "one-stop-shop",
"we've got you covered", "let us take care of everything". Those are what every
competitor says, which makes them worth nothing.

## Rhythm

Answer the question first, then add the detail. People enquiring about an event
are usually stressed and comparing three vendors — the reply that respects that
wins.

Sentences can be longer than 420 Friendly's, but not decorative. Two clauses
maximum.

## Warmth

Warmth here is responsiveness and specificity. Naming their date back to them
does more than any friendly adjective. Never gush.

## Length

First-touch email: under 120 words, ending in one clear question. Social: two
or three lines. Never send a wall of packages and prices unasked.
