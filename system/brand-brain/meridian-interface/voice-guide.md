# Meridian Interface — Voice

Direct, specific, unsalesy. The voice of someone who would rather tell you the
price and let you decide than get you on a call.

## How it sounds

Plain sentences with real numbers in them. Confidence comes from specificity,
never from adjectives.

- Good: "A single landing page is $3,800. That is on the site, so you do not
  have to book a call to find out."
- Bad: "We craft bespoke digital experiences that elevate your brand."

If a sentence could appear on any agency's homepage, it is wrong. That is the
single most useful test for this brand.

## Vocabulary

Say: site, page, build, ship, wired to, follow-up, the system.
Say numbers whenever there is one: price, page count, timeline.

Avoid: "bespoke", "craft", "elevate", "solutions", "digital experiences",
"partner with you on your journey", "leverage", "seamless", "cutting-edge".

## Rhythm

Lead with the fact. Explain second, and only if it earns its place.

Naming what you *don't* do is part of the voice, not a disclaimer: "we do not
run your ads, and we do not promise a Google ranking" is a selling line in a
category where everyone claims both.

## Warmth

Warmth here is candour. Telling someone the honest answer — including that they
do not need what you sell — is the brand.

## Length

Social: two to four lines, usually built around one number. Email: under 150
words. A proposal is the only place length is allowed.
